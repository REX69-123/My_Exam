05 // Comprehensive Final Reflection Essay

1. How has this subject helped you as an IT/CS student?

This course provided a fundamental perspective shift by bridging software engineering with cognitive ergonomics and instructional design. As a BSIT student, my technical education often focused primarily on writing code syntax, configuring databases, and building back-end logic. However, this course demonstrated that software is ultimately built for human interaction. Learning about cognitive load, structured goal setting, learning styles, and accessibility showed me how to design software applications that are intuitive, easy to adopt, and cognitively accessible for all users.

2. What significant knowledge, skills, attitudes, or values have you gained from the subject?

I acquired a thorough understanding of Bloom's Taxonomy, Cognitive Load Theory, Thorndike's Laws of Learning, and structured workspace design. On a skill level, I mastered breaking complex systems into modular steps and communicating technical ideas clearly. Most importantly, I developed an attitude of empathy in system design—realizing that technology should adjust to human cognitive limits rather than forcing users to adapt to confusing software.

3. How can you apply what you have learned to your academic life, future workplace, and professional responsibilities?

In my academic studies, I use content sequencing to digest complex computer science subjects systematically. In my future IT career, I will apply these skills to write clean technical documentation, run productive sprint retrospectives, and explain technical software architecture easily to non-technical client stakeholders.

4. How has this subject influenced or helped you develop your future career path?

This course showed me how important it is to be a technical professional who also understands human communication and system usability. It sparked my interest in career paths like Solutions Architect, UI/UX Systems Designer, and Technical Project Manager, proving that great IT leaders need both coding skills and clear communication abilities.

5. What specific lessons from this subject will you carry with you as a future IT/CS professional?

The main lesson I will always carry with me is the habit of breaking big problems into smaller, sequential steps while maintaining open feedback loops. Specific coding languages and tools will change over time, but knowing how to learn quickly, structure projects clearly, and communicate ideas effectively will always be useful in the IT industry.
